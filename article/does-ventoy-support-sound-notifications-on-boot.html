Does Ventoy Support Sound Notifications on Boot?

Ventoy does not natively support customized high-definition audio notifications, such as playing MP3 or WAV files, due to the lack of dedicated sound card drivers in pre-boot environments. However, because Ventoy is built on top of GRUB2, users can configure system beeps and simple melodies through the motherboard's internal PC speaker using custom GRUB configuration scripts.

Native Audio Limitations

Ventoy operates in a low-level environment—either Legacy BIOS or UEFI—before an operating system loads. In this pre-boot state, audio hardware such as integrated sound chips, USB audio interfaces, or HDMI audio channels are not initialized. Consequently, traditional sound notifications through standard speakers or headphones are not possible natively within Ventoy.

Using System Beeps via the PC Speaker

While high-definition sound is not supported, the underlying GRUB2 bootloader does support the classic internal PC speaker (motherboard buzzer). You can trigger sound beeps or simple 8-bit musical phrases on boot using GRUB’s built-in play command.

To implement a boot beep in Ventoy:

  1. Access the Ventoy Partition: Locate the primary data partition on your Ventoy USB drive.
  2. Create the Configuration Folder: Inside the ventoy directory, locate or create a custom configuration file named ventoy_grub.cfg.
  3. Add the Play Command: Insert the play command followed by the desired tempo, pitch (frequency in Hz), and duration.

For example, to produce a simple startup beep, you can add:

play 480 440 1

In this syntax, 480 represents the tempo, 440 is the frequency in Hertz (the note A4), and 1 is the note duration. You can string multiple notes together to create short alert melodies.

Hardware Prerequisites and Caveats

  • Internal Speaker Required: The GRUB play command requires a physical motherboard piezo buzzer or internal PC speaker connected to the motherboard header. Modern laptops and many desktop systems do not include this hardware, meaning no sound will be generated on those machines.
  • UEFI Compatibility: On some modern UEFI systems, legacy access to the programmable interval timer (PIT) used by the PC speaker may be restricted or disabled by the firmware, preventing the sound from playing even if an internal speaker is present.
  • No Official Plugin: Ventoy does not currently feature a dedicated option in its ventoy.json configuration file for sound alerts. All audio-based signaling must rely on manual extensions via ventoy_grub.cfg.
