Ventoy Sound Effects for Boot Menus

Ventoy is a popular open-source utility designed to create bootable USB drives simply by copying ISO, WIM, IMG, VHD(x), and EFI files directly to the storage media. While users frequently customize their boot menus with custom themes, high-resolution backgrounds, and icons, many wonder if audio feedback can be added to the interface. This article explains whether Ventoy supports sound effects when navigating or selecting boot items, the technical reasons behind its capabilities, and the extent of available audio workarounds.

Ventoy does not natively support sound effects for browsing, highlighting, or selecting boot menu options. The platform provides extensive customization through its Theme Plugin, but this plugin is strictly limited to graphical elements, such as background images, fonts, progress bars, and icon layouts. There is no built-in functionality to trigger WAV, MP3, or other audio files when pressing the arrow keys or hitting Enter.

The absence of sound effects stems from the early pre-boot environment in which Ventoy operates. Ventoy relies on a modified version of the GRUB2 bootloader. At the pre-boot stage, standard audio hardware drivers—such as those for modern Realtek codecs, HDMI audio, or USB sound cards—are not loaded. Because the environment lacks these complex drivers, standard media playback cannot be effortlessly tied to graphical user interface (GUI) events like key presses.

The underlying GRUB2 architecture does offer rudimentary support for the motherboard's legacy PC speaker via the GRUB play command. Through Ventoy's custom configuration extension (ventoy_grub.cfg), it is possible to execute a tune or series of beeps when the bootloader first initializes. This feature is primarily designed for accessibility, alerting visually impaired users that the system has successfully reached the boot prompt.

However, this PC speaker capability does not extend to real-time navigation sound effects. Ventoy's theme engine does not listen for user input events to trigger sound synthesis, and modern laptops and desktop motherboards frequently omit the internal piezo buzzer required to produce these basic tones.

Ventoy cannot play interactive sound effects on selecting or booting menu options. Customization within Ventoy remains focused entirely on visual theming and functional boot automation, with audio strictly limited to optional legacy motherboard beeps executed at initial load time.
