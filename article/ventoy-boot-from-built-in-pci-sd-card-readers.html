Ventoy Boot from Built-in PCI SD Card Readers

Ventoy generally does not support booting operating systems from SD cards inserted into built-in PCI or PCIe card readers. While Ventoy itself is capable of running from various storage media, the limitation stems from motherboard firmware constraints and a lack of early-stage drivers for PCI-based card reader controllers. This article explains the technical reasons behind this incompatibility, clarifies the distinction between PCI and USB card readers, and outlines the practical alternatives for booting with an SD card.

The Problem with Built-in PCI Card Readers

The primary obstacle is hardware and firmware architecture rather than Ventoy itself. Most built-in card readers in modern laptops are wired directly to the motherboard via the PCI Express (PCIe) bus rather than the internal USB bus.

For any storage device to boot, the computer's BIOS or UEFI firmware must contain a native driver to communicate with the host controller before any operating system loads. Standard UEFI firmware includes drivers for standard USB mass storage devices, SATA controllers, and NVMe drives, but it almost never includes drivers for proprietary PCIe SD card controllers manufactured by companies like Realtek, Genesys, or Broadcom. Because the BIOS cannot detect the PCIe-connected SD card as a bootable volume, it cannot launch the Ventoy bootloader stored on it.

Ventoy Runtime Driver Limitations

Even in rare cases where a manufacturer includes a UEFI driver that allows the firmware to initiate Ventoy from a PCIe SD reader, a secondary issue arises. Once the Ventoy bootloader initializes, it must locate and mount its secondary partition to display the ISO menu and read disk images. Ventoy's pre-boot environment (based on GRUB2) also lacks the proprietary PCIe SD card controller drivers. Consequently, the boot sequence will typically fail, stall, or display an error indicating that the Ventoy media cannot be found.

PCIe vs. Internal USB Card Readers

The behavior depends heavily on how the internal reader is wired to the motherboard:

  • PCIe-Connected Readers: These represent the vast majority of modern laptop readers. They deliver higher read and write speeds but cannot be booted via Ventoy due to the lack of pre-boot driver support.
  • Internally Connected USB Readers: Some older laptops, all-in-one PCs, and desktop front-panel hubs route their SD card slots through an internal USB 2.0 or USB 3.0 header. If a card reader is wired via internal USB, the system BIOS treats the SD card like a standard USB flash drive, allowing Ventoy to install, boot, and launch ISOs normally.

Recommended Alternatives

If you want to use Ventoy with an SD card, use one of the following methods:

  • External USB SD Card Reader: Insert the SD card into an inexpensive external USB adapter. The BIOS and Ventoy will recognize the adapter as a standard USB mass storage device, providing full boot functionality.
  • USB Flash Drive: Use a standard USB flash drive for Ventoy installations, which offers universal compatibility across virtually all UEFI and legacy BIOS systems.
  • Chainloading via GRUB/Clover: Advanced users can boot from a supported medium (like a small USB drive or local disk) containing dedicated drivers to initialize the PCIe card reader and then chainload the Ventoy installation on the SD card. However, this configuration is complex and prone to stability issues.
