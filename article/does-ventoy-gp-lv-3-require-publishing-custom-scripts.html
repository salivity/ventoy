Does Ventoy GPLv3 Require Publishing Custom Scripts?

Organizations using Ventoy under the GNU General Public License version 3 (GPLv3) are not legally required to publish or publicly disclose their customized internal scripts. Under the terms of the GPLv3, obligations to provide source code are triggered exclusively upon the distribution or "conveying" of software to third parties. Because private, in-house deployment and modification do not constitute external distribution, internal automation scripts, configuration files, and proprietary boot routines remain completely private to the organization.

The fundamental mechanism of the GPLv3 rests on the concept of conveying software. Section 2 of the license explicitly grants the right to make, run, and propagate covered works without conditions, provided the software is not conveyed to others. In a corporate or institutional environment, deploying Ventoy on internal drives for internal IT staff, system staging, or server provisioning is treated as private use. Because no transfer of software occurs outside the legal boundaries of the organization, no requirement to release source code or scripts is ever activated.

Furthermore, a clear distinction must be made between the Ventoy bootloader itself and the scripts an organization creates to use alongside it. Most customized scripts—such as unattended installation files (autounattend.xml, kickstart files, preseed files), custom shell scripts, and Ventoy configuration maps (ventoy.json)—are independent works. Even if an organization were to distribute bootable media externally, configuration data and separate scripts executing outside of Ventoy's codebase are generally considered independent assets rather than derivative works of the GPLv3-licensed bootloader engine.

An obligation to provide source code under GPLv3 arises only if two conditions are met simultaneously: the organization modifies Ventoy's underlying codebase (such as its core boot binaries or source files), and that modified software is distributed to clients, customers, or the public outside the organization. If modified binaries are distributed externally, the modified source code of Ventoy itself must be made available under the GPLv3.

For internal operations, automated hardware provisioning, and internal IT administration, organizations can freely develop, modify, and run custom Ventoy scripts without any legal risk or requirement to share their proprietary intellectual property.
