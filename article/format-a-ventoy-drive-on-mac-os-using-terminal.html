Format a Ventoy Drive on macOS Using Terminal

This guide explains how to format a Ventoy USB drive on macOS using the command-line interface. Ventoy creates hidden and protected partitions that often fail to format properly through the standard macOS Disk Utility interface. Following the steps below will show you how to identify the drive identifier, unmount the volumes, and run the diskutil command to completely wipe and restore the drive to a standard filesystem.

Step 1: Open Terminal

Open the Terminal application by pressing Cmd + Space to launch Spotlight, typing Terminal, and pressing Enter.

Step 2: Identify the Ventoy Drive

Connect your Ventoy USB drive to your Mac and list all connected storage devices by running:

diskutil list

Review the output to find your Ventoy drive. It typically displays two partitions: an exFAT or NTFS partition alongside a small VTOYEFI partition. Locate the disk node identifier corresponding to the whole drive (for example, /dev/disk2 or /dev/disk3).

Note: Ensure you identify the correct disk number to prevent accidental data loss on your internal drive.

Step 3: Unmount the Drive

Before formatting, you must unmount all active partitions on the drive. Replace diskN with your actual disk identifier (e.g., disk2):

diskutil unmountDisk /dev/diskN

Terminal should return a confirmation stating: Unmount of all volumes on diskN was successful.

Step 4: Erase and Reformat the Drive

To completely remove the Ventoy bootloader, partition table, and data, format the entire disk using diskutil eraseDisk. Choose the command that matches your target filesystem and partition scheme:

For exFAT with an MBR partition scheme (compatible with Windows and macOS):

diskutil eraseDisk ExFAT "USBDrive" MBR /dev/diskN

For MS-DOS (FAT32) with an MBR partition scheme:

diskutil eraseDisk FAT32 "USBDrive" MBR /dev/diskN

For APFS with a GUID partition scheme (macOS only):

diskutil eraseDisk APFS "USBDrive" GPT /dev/diskN

Replace USBDrive with your preferred drive label and /dev/diskN with your specific disk identifier.

Alternative: Format Only the Ventoy Storage Partition

If your goal is to keep the Ventoy bootable environment intact while clearing all stored ISO files, reformat only the data partition rather than the whole disk.

  1. Identify the partition slice (usually diskNs1) from diskutil list.
  2. Format only that specific volume:
diskutil eraseVolume ExFAT "Ventoy" /dev/diskNs1

Step 5: Verify the Drive

Once the process finishes, verify the new partition structure:

diskutil list /dev/diskN

The output will confirm that the Ventoy partitions have been replaced by a single, clean partition layout. You can now use or safely eject the drive using:

diskutil eject /dev/diskN
