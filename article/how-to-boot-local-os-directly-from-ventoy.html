How to Boot Local OS Directly from Ventoy

When booting from a Ventoy USB drive, you do not need to remove the drive or reconfigure your computer's BIOS/UEFI boot priority just to access your internal drive. Ventoy includes integrated local boot functions designed to detect and chainload your computer's existing operating system directly from its interface. This guide explains the exact methods and keyboard shortcuts required to boot your local Windows or Linux installation straight from the Ventoy menu.

Method 1: Search and Boot Local Windows (F4 Key)

If your local operating system is Windows, Ventoy provides a dedicated shortcut to detect and run the internal Windows bootloader:

  1. Turn on your computer and boot into the Ventoy main menu.
  2. Press the F4 key on your keyboard.
  3. Ventoy will scan all internal hard drives and solid-state drives for valid Windows boot files (bootmgr for Legacy BIOS or bootmgfw.efi for UEFI).
  4. Select the detected local drive or partition from the resulting list and press Enter to start Windows.

Method 2: Use the Localboot Menu (F5 Key)

If you are running Linux, a non-Windows OS, or if the F4 method does not locate your drive, use the general Localboot utility:

  1. On the main Ventoy screen, press the F5 key to open the Tools menu.
  2. Navigate down to the Localboot option and press Enter.
  3. You will see a list of detected local boot paths and physical drives.
  4. Select your internal disk (e.g., hd0, hd1, or specific UEFI boot entries) and press Enter to chainload the local operating system.

Method 3: Exit to BIOS/UEFI Boot Selection

If Ventoy cannot chainload your specific partition configuration, you can exit the environment directly:

  1. In the Ventoy menu, press the Esc key.
  2. Confirm the exit prompt if one appears.
  3. Depending on your motherboard firmware, exiting will either fall back to the next device in your system's boot priority list (your internal drive) or return you to the motherboard's quick boot menu, allowing you to select your local drive manually.
