Can Ventoy Update Motherboard BIOS Directly?

Ventoy cannot natively flash raw motherboard BIOS files (such as .bin, .cap, or .rom) directly from its boot menu. Because Ventoy is a bootable image launcher rather than a flashing utility, it does not include the hardware-level tools required to write firmware to your motherboard. However, Ventoy can facilitate the BIOS update process by booting bootable environments—such as FreeDOS, Linux Live environments, or UEFI shell utilities—that contain the necessary flashing software.

Why Ventoy Cannot Flash BIOS Files Directly

Motherboard firmware updates require dedicated flashing software that interacts directly with your system's EEPROM chip. Ventoy operates as a multi-boot loader designed to read and boot disk images (like ISO, IMG, or EFI files). Raw BIOS binaries are simply data files; without an executable updater designed for your specific motherboard chipset, Ventoy has no mechanism to execute the update.

Methods to Update BIOS Using Ventoy

While Ventoy cannot flash files directly, you can use it to boot intermediate environments that perform the update:

  • FreeDOS Images: For older motherboards or specific enterprise hardware that uses DOS-based flashers, you can place a bootable FreeDOS ISO onto your Ventoy drive. After booting into FreeDOS via the Ventoy menu, you can run the manufacturer's executable (e.g., flash.bat or afudos.exe) alongside the BIOS binary.
  • Manufacturer Bootable ISOs: Manufacturers like Lenovo, Dell, and HP often distribute BIOS updates as self-contained, bootable ISO files. Ventoy can launch these ISOs directly, allowing the vendor's internal Linux- or DOS-based updater to handle the process automatically.
  • UEFI Shell and .efi Updaters: Ventoy can launch standalone .efi executables. If your motherboard manufacturer provides an EFI-based flash tool, you can launch the UEFI environment through Ventoy and execute the update command.

Built-in Motherboard Flash Utilities (EZ Flash, M-Flash, Q-Flash)

Modern motherboards from manufacturers like ASUS, MSI, Gigabyte, and ASRock feature proprietary flashing utilities integrated into the BIOS setup screen (such as ASUS EZ Flash or MSI M-Flash).

These tools bypass the bootloader entirely:

  1. You reboot your PC and enter the motherboard's BIOS setup utility (usually by pressing Delete or F2).
  2. The internal utility scans attached storage devices for the raw BIOS file.
  3. Because Ventoy creates a standard data partition (typically formatted in exFAT or FAT32) to hold your files, your motherboard’s built-in tool may still be able to read the raw BIOS file stored on the drive.

In this scenario, Ventoy itself is not performing the update or even running; the USB drive is simply functioning as standard storage for the motherboard's internal updater.

Best Practices and Precautions

Interrupting a BIOS flash can permanently damage your motherboard. While using Ventoy to host files or boot live operating systems is functional, the safest method for updating modern motherboards remains using a dedicated USB flash drive formatted in FAT32, running the update through the motherboard’s native BIOS utility rather than through any third-party bootloader.
