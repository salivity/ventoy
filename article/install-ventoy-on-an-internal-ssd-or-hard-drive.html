Install Ventoy on an Internal SSD or Hard Drive

Ventoy is widely recognized as a tool for creating bootable USB flash drives, but it can also be installed directly onto an internal solid-state drive (SSD) or hard disk drive (HDD). While Ventoy deliberately hides internal disks by default to protect users from accidental data loss, changing a simple setting allows you to turn any internal drive into a high-speed, multi-boot storage unit capable of launching ISO, WIM, and IMG files without external media.

How Ventoy Works on Internal Drives

Ventoy functions on an internal SATA or NVMe drive just as it does on a USB stick. During installation, it divides the target drive into two partitions:

  1. VTOYEFI Partition: A small, hidden FAT-formatted system partition that contains the bootloader (supporting both legacy BIOS and UEFI modes).
  2. Data Partition: The remaining drive space formatted as exFAT by default (though NTFS, ext4, or other file systems are supported), where you paste your bootable ISO files.

Once configured, your system's motherboard can boot directly to the internal drive, presenting the standard Ventoy menu to launch any operating system image stored on it.

How to Enable Internal Drive Installation

Because an installation formats the target drive and wipes all existing data, internal disks are protected by default. To make an internal drive visible:

  1. Open the Ventoy installation program on your computer.
  2. Click on the Option menu at the top.
  3. Check the box labeled Show all devices.
  4. Return to the device dropdown list and select your internal SSD or HDD.
  5. Click Install to partition the drive and set up the Ventoy boot environment.

Key Advantages

  • Superior Read Speeds: Internal SATA and NVMe drives offer dramatically faster read and write speeds than standard USB flash drives. Operating system installers and live environments load significantly faster.
  • Large Storage Capacity: Internal drives generally offer larger capacities at a lower cost, allowing you to store dozens of OS images, diagnostic utilities, and large backup files on a single machine.
  • Convenience: An internal multi-boot drive eliminates the need to carry or keep track of physical USB drives for maintenance, testing, or multi-OS environments.

Risks and Limitations

  • Total Data Erasure: Standard installation overwrites the drive's partition table. Installing Ventoy on the wrong internal drive will erase your existing operating system and personal data.
  • Single-Drive Dual-Booting Complications: While Ventoy can reserve non-partitioned space at the end of the disk for a secondary OS installation, running both a primary operating system and Ventoy on the exact same physical drive requires advanced partition management. Ventoy is best deployed on a dedicated secondary internal drive rather than sharing a drive with your main operating system.
